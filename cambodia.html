Basic information

Quick Facts

Cambodia is a Southeast Asian nation bordered by Thailand, Laos, and Vietnam. Phnom Penh is the capital and largest city, and the country has a population of around 17 million people.

The Khmer Empire, which reached its peak between the 9th and 15th centuries, left behind a legacy of temple complexes that define the country's identity today.

Landscapes & Locations

Cambodia's geography ranges from the flat plains of the Tonlé Sap lake — Southeast Asia's largest freshwater lake — to the forested highlands of the Cardamom Mountains.

Coastal towns like Sihanoukville offer beaches and ocean scenery, while Phnom Penh blends modern life with French colonial architecture.

Angkor Wat

Temple of the Khmer Empire

Angkor Wat is the largest religious monument in the world, originally built as a Hindu temple in the early 12th century before gradually converting to Buddhism.

The wider Angkor archaeological park spans over 400 square kilometres and contains hundreds of temples and ancient structures.

Cultural heritage

Tradition and Resilience

Cambodian culture has survived immense hardship and continues to be expressed through classical Khmer dance, shadow puppetry, silk weaving, and temple festivals.

Buddhism is central to everyday life, with monasteries serving as community hubs across the country.
